# Customs and duties

Shipments crossing a border need a clear goods description, declared value, country of origin, and the receiver's contact details. Kestrel Freight can check that these fields are present before tendering a shipment, but the shipper remains responsible for accurate declarations and any permits required for the goods.

Customs duties and import taxes are set by the destination authority. They are not included in Kestrel's transport quote unless the written quote explicitly says duties paid. When charges are billed to the receiver, the receiver may need to pay the authority or its appointed broker before release. Kestrel does not estimate an official duty amount from a product name alone.

For a customs review, send a commercial invoice, shipment reference, and any requested permit or product certificate to your account contact. The review usually adds 1 to 2 business days after the authority starts its inspection, though the authority may take longer or request more documents.

Do not mark commercial goods as gifts to avoid charges. If a declaration needs correction, contact Kestrel before departure. A correction after arrival can delay release and may incur charges from third parties.
